# Part F: Review after stakeholder context

The round-1 allocation totals 50 points: Must 29, Should 10, Could 11. Its importance judgements were assumptions rather than stakeholder-backed decisions. The first-round files were saved before the stakeholder notes were read; their hashes are in `first_round_manifest.json`.

| Round-1 decision | Supplied stakeholder evidence | Review |
|---|---|---|
| SF-06 Apply a promo code at checkout — Could | Founder: “Without promo codes there is no launch campaign.” | Promote to Must for the stated launch campaign. |
| SF-13 Pay cash on delivery — Must | Operations: “About 70% of first-time food delivery customers in the city pay cash on delivery.” | Retain Must; the note now supports the earlier cash-access assumption. |
| SF-10 Rate and review a restaurant after delivery — Could | Marketing: “Ratings and reviews are our main trust signal against the established apps.” | Promote to Should: important trust context, with no explicit prohibition on launching without it. |

SF-11 Schedule an order for a later time moves from Should to Could to keep the Should allocation focused on the newly supported trust priority. That displacement is an assumption: immediate ordering remains a workaround, and the notes do not directly mention scheduling. SF-04 Pay by card or e-wallet remains Must under the original online-payment launch assumption; the 70% cash note does not establish that every customer can use cash.

Quality review rejected making SF-10 Rate and review a restaurant after delivery Must merely to obtain an exact percentage split: the note describes its importance but does not explicitly make it a launch blocker. The final 64/20/16 mix is accepted as approximate, with the deviation stated. The launch-policy assumptions should still be confirmed in a real project.

The changed story set and both rounds' totals are recorded in `moscow.md`. Every story is still classified exactly once; no story-point estimate has been changed.
