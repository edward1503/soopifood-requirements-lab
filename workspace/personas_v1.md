# SoopiFood: Restaurant Manager Personas — Draft 1

**BASED ON SIMULATED TUTORIAL DATA — NOT VALIDATED USER RESEARCH**

These three composite personas synthesise `research_notes.md`, containing fictional interviews I1–I8 and fictional observations O1–O2. Names are invented labels for the composites, not additional participants. Each persona groups related goals and working patterns; its combined attributes do not describe a single interviewed person or establish how common that pattern is. No demographic details have been added.

Source tags refer to the interview or observation IDs in the notes. O1 is paired with I1, and O2 with I4; these scenes are not independent real-world corroboration. Context-specific differences are identified below rather than treated as universal behaviour.

## 1. Sam, the Manager Covering the Counter

Sam represents managers whose attention is divided between order handling, collection and other service duties. The central concern is keeping orders complete and work accounted for when customer interactions or changing duties interrupt a check. [I1, I5, O1]

### Goals

- Have complete, correct orders leave the restaurant, including when checks are interrupted. [I1, I5]
- Finish service without a kitchen backlog. [I1]
- Give collection customers and dining-room guests attentive service when both need staff time. [I5]
- Maintain continuity of order handling when colleagues cover one another or change duties. [I5]

### Behaviours

- Moves between serving customers, checking incoming work, packing and handing over orders. [I1, I5, O1]
- Combines an existing ordering device with paper tickets and spoken kitchen updates. [I1, I5, O1]
- Checks bag contents and collection references before handover; in the observed fictional interruption, restarts the item check after serving another customer. [I1, O1]
- Uses the retained ticket and confirmation from the kitchen when responding to a missing-item call. [I1]

### Pain points

- Repeated counter interruptions make it harder to remember which bags have already been checked. [I1]
- Shared counter space can leave an incomplete bag looking ready for collection; one reported near-miss involved drinks that had not yet been added. [I5]
- When duties move between colleagues, establishing who last handled an outstanding order takes extra attention. [I5]
- Access to an existing order can be interrupted: I1 reports connection problems, while O1 depicts a loading screen without establishing its cause or whether any unseen orders are missing. [I1, O1]

### Context

- In the takeaway setting, the manager shares the dinner shift with two kitchen colleagues and covers the customer-facing counter. [I1, O1]
- Work can arrive through delivery platforms, telephone calls and walk-in customers. [I1]
- In the full-service setting, delivery supervision competes with dining-room duties, and information is often read while standing between other tasks. [I5]
- Physical collection space is limited in the full-service setting, and the manager does not control decisions about reallocating floor space. [I5]

## 2. Alex, the Manager Coordinating Preparation and Pickup

Alex represents managers who can delegate routine order handling and focus on how preparation and collection fit together. Existing receipt processes or advance planning often work adequately, while clustered orders, early arrivals and changed arrangements require coordination with the kitchen. [I4, I6, O2]

### Goals

- Have complete food ready in an acceptable condition close to collection time. [I4, I6]
- Keep collection waits reasonable while preserving the completeness of the order. [I4]
- Keep the kitchen's workload manageable through service. [I6]
- Have staff work from the same current collection arrangement when plans change. [I6]

### Behaviours

- Leaves routine receipt, packing and reference checks to colleagues rather than personally handling every order. [I4, I6, O2]
- In the dedicated pickup setting, relies on the coordinator to obtain a kitchen update before giving a rider an approximate wait. [I4, O2]
- Checks proposed collection changes with the kitchen before confirming them to a customer. [I6]
- Preserves the final completeness check; in the dedicated pickup scene, the packer keeps a bag open until its remaining item arrives. [I4, O2]

### Pain points

- Several large orders close together put pressure on oven capacity and preparation sequencing, even when order receipt works reliably. [I4]
- Repeated rider requests for updates interrupt the coordinator's other order-handling work. [I4, O2]
- A late change can leave kitchen staff following a preparation list that no longer reflects the current arrangement. [I6]
- After food leaves the restaurant, the manager has incomplete information about the delivery sequence and cannot always establish what caused a complaint. [I4]

### Context

- The dedicated pickup setting separates the manager, order coordinator, packer and kitchen roles. [I4, O2]
- In the advance-order setting, many orders have agreed collection windows, with fewer same-evening orders. [I6]
- Existing information sources include order screens, printed tickets and a kitchen board in one setting, and preparation lists, labels and telephone messages in the other. [I4, I6]
- Preparation estimates can change with the oven queue; the manager does not treat them as guaranteed completion times. [I4]

## 3. Morgan, the Manager Maintaining Consistency Across Shifts

Morgan represents managers concerned with consistent interpretation, responsibility and exception decisions across colleagues and shifts. Routine work can be delegated or handled through familiar practices, but unclear notes, incomplete handovers and unusual requests need particular attention. [I2, I3, I7, I8]

### Goals

- Have orders understood consistently by experienced and newer colleagues. [I3]
- Provide dependable service regardless of which colleagues are on shift. [I8]
- Have staff resolve matters within their authority and escalate those that need the manager's decision. [I7]
- Preserve dependable, familiar routines while making responsibility clear when questions arise. [I2, I8]

### Behaviours

- Assigns counter coverage or relies on a delivery lead, becoming involved in exceptions rather than every routine collection. [I3, I7]
- Seeks clarification of unfamiliar abbreviations and preparation notes before staff act on an uncertain interpretation. [I3]
- Reviews unresolved cases at shift handover and asks what colleagues have already checked when an issue is escalated. [I7]
- Checks an unusual customer request with the kitchen before explaining the available options, and explains exception decisions to colleagues at handover. [I8]

### Pain points

- Abbreviations familiar to experienced staff can be interpreted differently by newer colleagues. [I3]
- Responsibility for outstanding queries can become unclear during a shift change. [I3]
- Existing records do not always explain who made an exception decision, so simultaneous escalations require reconstructing events. [I7]
- Occasional staff may miss explanations given at another handover; in one reported case, colleagues disagreed about who could authorise a change after preparation. [I8]

### Context

- The staffing pattern includes experienced and recently hired colleagues rotating through counter duties, or occasional colleagues working selected shifts. [I3, I8]
- In the larger-restaurant setting, a delivery lead coordinates routine pickup while the duty manager oversees the whole service. [I7]
- Some customer questions reach staff through an ordering service, and direct clarification is not always immediately available. [I3]
- Familiar telephone and paper routines are reported as adequate for ordinary work in some settings; the expressed priority is clearer responsibility or more consistent exception handling. [I2, I8]

## What makes the personas distinct

Sam's defining concern is continuity of hands-on order checks amid competing service tasks. [I1, I5, O1] Alex's is coordination between preparation readiness and collection, including when routine receipt and delegation already work. [I4, I6, O2] Morgan's is consistent understanding and decisions across people, shifts and exceptions, while preserving effective routines and delegation. [I2, I3, I7, I8]
