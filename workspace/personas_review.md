# SoopiFood: Review of Persona Draft 1

**BASED ON SIMULATED TUTORIAL DATA — NOT VALIDATED USER RESEARCH**

## Review basis and verdict

Reviewed `personas_v1.md` against `research_notes.md` and `starter/prompts/03_personas.md`. The assessment checks whether the draft follows its fictional source material; it does not validate the personas against real restaurant managers.

The draft is mostly supported and meets the requested structure. No invented demographics, numerical targets or proposed product features were found. The main weaknesses concern the scope of composite claims, generalisation from individual incidents and a few goals phrased around preserving practices rather than their intended outcomes. These are qualified or reworded in `personas_v2.md`.

## Overlap and distinctions

| Persona | Central situation | What distinguishes the need |
|---|---|---|
| Sam | Current order checks compete with counter and customer tasks. | Continuity of hands-on work after interruptions, especially before releasing a bag. [I1/Q4/Q9, O1-E3/E7] |
| Alex | Preparation progress and collection arrangements must fit together. | Coordination of kitchen readiness and pickup expectations, including when receipt already works. [I4/Q3–Q6/Q9, I6/Q3–Q9, O2-E3–E6] |
| Morgan | Different colleagues interpret requests, hand over work and decide exceptions. | Consistency of understanding and responsibility across people and shifts. [I3/Q3–Q9, I7/Q3–Q9, I8/Q5/Q8–Q9] |

Accurate handover and clear responsibility remain shared needs. These personas are overlapping tutorial perspectives, not mutually exclusive categories or measured segments of restaurant managers.

## Recommended persona for the restaurant-tablet exercise

**Recommend Sam, using I1/O1 as the primary working context.** I1/Q2–Q3 directly describes a manager receiving an order on an existing tablet and combining it with kitchen information. I1/Q4/Q9 and O1-E3/E7 provide concrete examples of interrupted checking and preventing an incomplete handover. This gives the next exercise a clear frontline actor and a sequence of restaurant-side activities to explore.

I5 adds useful context about brief attention and changing responsibilities, but its dining-room and floor-space details should be carried into a later story only when that variant is deliberately included. Alex is useful for delegated pickup and preparation coordination; Morgan is useful for handover and exception-consistency scenarios. Sam is recommended for the direct manager/tablet interaction in this exercise, rather than because the simulated material establishes greater prevalence or importance.

Before functional requirements are drafted, review `personas_v2.md`, confirm the chosen context, write that persona's user stories and prepare W1–W4 prototype walkthrough notes. Preserve the simulation label if those walkthroughs are simulated. The current notes leave menu-availability behaviour, acceptance rules, timing thresholds, and what happens to orders during interrupted access unresolved; those details need explicit source input or `[TBC]` treatment rather than being inferred from this persona review.
