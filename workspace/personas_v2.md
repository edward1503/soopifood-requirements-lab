# SoopiFood: Restaurant Manager Personas — Draft 2

**BASED ON SIMULATED TUTORIAL DATA — NOT VALIDATED USER RESEARCH**

These three composite personas are revised against the fictional interviews I1–I8 and fictional observations O1–O2 in `research_notes.md`. Their names are invented labels, with no added demographic details. They organise related needs for this tutorial; combined attributes do not establish that one participant had all of them, how common a pattern is, or how a future product should work.

Sam is primarily anchored in I1/O1, with I5 as a full-service variant. Alex is primarily anchored in I4/O2, with I6 as a scheduled-order variant. Morgan is primarily anchored in I3, with related cases from I2, I7 and I8. Variant-specific claims below retain their setting. O1/I1 and O2/I4 are paired fictional scenes, not independent corroboration. The review and source locators are in `personas_review.md`.

## 1. Sam, the Manager Covering the Counter

Sam represents a manager who personally handles incoming work, bag checks and collections while serving customers at a takeaway counter. [I1, O1] The related full-service variant adds dining-room duties and changes in who is responsible for outstanding orders. [I5]

### Goals

- Have complete, correct orders leave the restaurant despite competing service demands. [I1, I5]
- Finish service without a kitchen backlog. [I1]
- In the full-service variant, have both dining-room guests and collection customers receive attentive service. [I5]
- In the full-service variant, maintain continuity of order handling when colleagues cover one another or change duties. [I5]

### Behaviours

- Moves between serving counter customers, checking incoming work, packing and handing over orders. [I1, O1]
- Combines an existing ordering device with paper tickets and spoken kitchen updates. [I1, I5, O1]
- Checks bag contents and collection references before handover; O1 depicts one item check being restarted after an interruption. [I1, O1]
- For one reported missing-item call, checked the retained ticket and confirmed with the kitchen before arranging a replacement. [I1]

### Pain points

- Repeated counter interruptions make it harder to remember which bags have already been checked. [I1]
- In the full-service variant, shared counter space contributed to a near-miss involving a bag whose drinks had not yet been added. [I5]
- In the full-service variant, changing duties creates extra work to establish who last handled an outstanding order. [I5]
- Reports occasional connection problems when accessing existing orders; the paired fictional observation shows a loading screen but does not establish its cause or whether unseen orders are missing. [I1, O1]

### Context

- In the primary takeaway setting, the manager shares the described dinner shift with two kitchen colleagues and covers the counter. [I1, O1]
- Work arrives through delivery platforms, telephone calls and walk-in customers. [I1]
- In the full-service variant, delivery supervision competes with dining-room duties, and information is often read while standing between other tasks. [I5]
- In the full-service variant, collection space is limited and layout decisions belong to the owner. [I5]

## 2. Alex, the Manager Coordinating Preparation and Pickup

Alex represents a manager overseeing preparation and collection while a coordinator and packer handle routine pickup work. [I4, O2] The related scheduled-order variant adds the need to reconcile changed collection arrangements with an advance preparation plan. [I6]

### Goals

- Have complete food ready in an acceptable condition close to collection time. [I4, I6]
- Keep collection waits reasonable while preserving the completeness of the order. [I4]
- In the scheduled-order variant, keep the kitchen's workload manageable through service. [I6]
- In the scheduled-order variant, have preparation and collection follow a shared, current arrangement. [I6]

### Behaviours

- In the dedicated pickup setting, leaves routine receipt, packing and collection checks to the coordinator and packer rather than personally handling every order. [I4, O2]
- In that workflow, the coordinator obtains kitchen updates and communicates approximate waits to riders; the manager oversees exceptions. [I4, O2]
- In the scheduled-order variant, checks proposed collection changes with the kitchen before confirming them to the customer. [I6]
- Preserves the final completeness check; the dedicated pickup scene depicts the packer keeping a bag open until its remaining item arrives. [I4, O2]

### Pain points

- Several large orders close together put pressure on oven capacity and preparation sequencing, even when order receipt works reliably. [I4]
- Repeated rider requests for updates interrupt the coordinator's other order-handling work. [I4, O2]
- In the scheduled-order variant, messages arriving after a preparation list is printed require an explicit check with the kitchen. [I6]
- After food leaves the restaurant, has incomplete information about the delivery sequence and cannot always establish what caused a complaint. [I4]

### Context

- The primary setting separates the manager, order coordinator, packer and kitchen roles. [I4, O2]
- In the scheduled-order variant, many orders have agreed collection windows, with fewer same-evening orders. [I6]
- Uses order screens, printed tickets and a kitchen board in the primary setting; the scheduled-order variant uses preparation lists, labels and telephone messages. [I4, I6]
- Preparation estimates in the primary setting can change with the oven queue and are not treated as guaranteed completion times. [I4]

## 3. Morgan, the Manager Maintaining Consistency Across Shifts

Morgan represents a manager seeking consistent interpretation and clear responsibility as experienced and newer colleagues rotate through service duties. [I3] Related cases show the same concern in delegated exception decisions, occasional staffing and the ownership of questions within familiar telephone-order routines. [I2, I7, I8]

### Goals

- Have orders understood consistently by experienced and newer colleagues. [I3]
- Provide dependable service regardless of which colleagues are on shift. [I8]
- Have staff resolve matters within their authority and recognise when a manager's decision is needed. [I7]
- Have dependable handling of ordinary orders and clear responsibility for questions that arise. [I2, I8]

### Behaviours

- In the rotating-staff setting, assigns counter coverage and takes over when a colleague is occupied; in the larger-restaurant variant, relies on a delivery lead for routine work. [I3, I7]
- Seeks clarification of unfamiliar abbreviations and preparation notes before staff act on an uncertain interpretation. [I3]
- In the delegated setting, reviews unresolved cases at shift handover and asks what colleagues have already checked when an issue is escalated. [I7]
- In the occasional-staff case, checks an unusual customer request with the kitchen; one reported exception decision was explained to colleagues at the next handover. [I8]

### Pain points

- Abbreviations familiar to experienced staff can be interpreted differently by newer colleagues. [I3]
- Responsibility for outstanding queries can become unclear during a shift change. [I3]
- In the delegated setting, existing records do not always explain who made an exception decision, so simultaneous escalations require reconstructing events. [I7]
- In the occasional-staff case, colleagues disagreed about authority to change a prepared order, and the manager has not checked whether every occasional colleague heard the later explanation. [I8]

### Context

- The primary setting has experienced and recently hired colleagues rotating through counter duties; a related setting includes occasional colleagues working selected shifts. [I3, I8]
- In the larger-restaurant variant, a delivery lead coordinates routine pickup while the duty manager oversees the whole service. [I7]
- In the primary setting, some customer questions arrive through an ordering service and direct clarification is not always immediately available. [I3]
- In the telephone-order cases, familiar paper and telephone routines are reported as adequate for ordinary work; responsibility and uncommon situations remain priorities. [I2, I8]

## What makes the personas distinct

Sam focuses on continuity of hands-on order checks when immediate service tasks interrupt one another. [I1, I5, O1] Alex focuses on fitting preparation readiness to collection arrangements, with routine pickup roles already delegated. [I4, I6, O2] Morgan focuses on consistent interpretation and responsibility across people, shifts and exceptions. [I2, I3, I7, I8] Complete orders and clear responsibility are shared needs; the personas differ in the circumstances that make those needs difficult to meet.
